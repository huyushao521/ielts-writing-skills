## Essay 1: Education — University Education (Band 8.0)

**Prompt:** Some people think that university education should be free for everyone. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

**Sample Essay:**

The question of whether university education should be provided at no cost to all students has sparked considerable debate. While some advocate for tuition-free higher education as a fundamental right, I firmly disagree with this proposition, as it would place an unsustainable financial burden on governments and potentially devalue academic qualifications.

The primary reason why free university education is impractical is the enormous cost involved. Higher education institutions require substantial funding to maintain facilities, pay qualified academic staff, and support research initiatives. If governments were to absorb these costs entirely, the financial strain would be immense, potentially diverting resources from other critical sectors such as healthcare and infrastructure. For instance, in countries like Germany, where university education is largely free, the system relies on significantly higher tax rates to sustain funding, which not all economies can support.

Furthermore, making university education universally free could lead to academic inflation, whereby degrees lose their value in the job market. When access to higher education is unrestricted, the number of graduates may exceed the available positions requiring such qualifications. Consequently, individuals would invest years of study only to find themselves competing for roles that do not necessitate a degree, rendering their academic efforts redundant. This phenomenon is already observable in some nations where degree saturation has diminished the competitive advantage that a university qualification once provided.

A more viable alternative would be a means-tested system, wherein government subsidies are directed towards students from disadvantaged backgrounds, while those who can afford to contribute are expected to do so. This approach ensures that higher education remains accessible to talented individuals regardless of their financial circumstances, without imposing an unmanageable burden on public finances.

In conclusion, while the intention behind free university education is commendable, its implementation would be economically unsustainable and could diminish the value of academic qualifications. A targeted subsidy system represents a more balanced and pragmatic approach.

*(298 words)*

**Band 8.0 features:**
- Clear position from the start, maintained throughout
- Well-developed arguments with specific examples (Germany, academic inflation)
- Advanced vocabulary: "unsustainable financial burden," "academic inflation," "means-tested system"
- Complex grammar: conditional ("If governments were to..."), passive ("would be directed"), relative clauses
- Logical paragraphing: Intro → Cost argument → Value argument → Alternative solution → Conclusion
- Cohesive devices: "Furthermore," "Consequently," "A more viable alternative," "In conclusion"

## Essay 2: Technology — Social Media (Band 7.5)

**Prompt:** Many people believe that social media has a negative impact on society. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

**Sample Essay:**

The proliferation of social media platforms has transformed the way people communicate and share information. While some argue that these platforms have detrimental effects on society, I believe that although social media presents certain challenges, its overall impact is largely positive.

Admittedly, social media is not without its drawbacks. One significant concern is the spread of misinformation. The ease with which content can be shared means that false or misleading information can reach a vast audience within minutes, potentially causing public confusion or even panic. Additionally, excessive use of social media has been linked to mental health issues, particularly among young people. The constant exposure to curated lifestyles can foster feelings of inadequacy and anxiety, as individuals compare their own lives to idealized representations online.

However, the benefits of social media are substantial. Primarily, these platforms have democratized communication, allowing individuals to connect across geographical and cultural boundaries. This has facilitated the exchange of ideas, promoted cross-cultural understanding, and enabled social movements to gain momentum. For instance, platforms like Twitter have been instrumental in raising awareness about social justice issues, enabling marginalized voices to reach a global audience. Furthermore, social media has created new economic opportunities, particularly for small businesses and independent creators who can now reach customers directly without the need for traditional marketing channels.

In conclusion, while social media does pose challenges such as the spread of misinformation and potential mental health impacts, its ability to connect people globally and create economic opportunities makes it a net positive force in society. The key lies not in restricting these platforms, but in promoting digital literacy and responsible usage.

*(281 words)*

**Band 7.5 features:**
- Balanced discussion with clear own opinion
- Less common vocabulary: "proliferation," "democratized communication," "curated lifestyles," "marginalized voices"
- Good cohesion: "Admittedly," "However," "Furthermore," "In conclusion"
- Complex structures: relative clauses, conditional reasoning
- Specific example (Twitter and social justice)

## Essay 3: Environment — Climate Change (Band 7.0)

**Prompt:** Some people think that individuals cannot do much to protect the environment, and that governments and large companies should be responsible. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

**Sample Essay:**

Environmental protection has become one of the most pressing issues of our time. While some argue that individual actions are insignificant compared to the efforts of governments and corporations, I disagree with this view, as personal choices play a crucial role in addressing environmental challenges.

It is true that governments and large companies have a greater capacity to implement large-scale changes. Governments can enact legislation to reduce carbon emissions, invest in renewable energy infrastructure, and impose penalties on polluting industries. Similarly, corporations have the resources to adopt sustainable manufacturing practices and develop eco-friendly technologies. These actions undoubtedly have a more immediate and far-reaching impact than any single individual's efforts.

However, dismissing the role of individuals would be a mistake. Consumer behavior drives market demand, which in turn influences corporate decisions. When individuals choose to purchase environmentally friendly products, reduce their use of single-use plastics, or opt for public transportation, they collectively exert significant pressure on businesses to adopt greener practices. For example, the growing consumer demand for sustainable products has prompted numerous multinational companies to revise their environmental policies.

Furthermore, individual actions contribute to raising public awareness, which is essential for generating political will. Governments are more likely to prioritize environmental policies when their citizens demonstrate concern through their daily choices and voting behavior.

In conclusion, while governments and corporations possess the resources to implement large-scale environmental measures, individual actions are far from insignificant. Personal choices influence market demand and political priorities, making them an indispensable component of environmental protection efforts.

*(265 words)*

**Band 7.0 features:**
- Clear position with concession
- Logical argument structure
- Good use of cohesive devices
- Some less common vocabulary: "far-reaching impact," "exert significant pressure," "indispensable component"
- Could improve: more complex sentence variety, more specific examples

## Essay 4: Society — Aging Population (Band 8.0)

**Prompt:** The average age of the population in many countries is increasing. Do the advantages of this trend outweigh the disadvantages?

**Sample Essay:**

The demographic shift towards an older population is a phenomenon being experienced by numerous developed nations. While this trend presents certain challenges, I believe the disadvantages are significant enough to outweigh the advantages.

One acknowledged advantage of an aging population is the preservation of wisdom and experience within society. Older individuals often possess valuable professional expertise and life experience that can benefit younger generations. Furthermore, many retirees contribute to their communities through volunteering and childcare, providing essential support that might otherwise require paid services.

Nevertheless, the drawbacks associated with an aging population are profound. The most pressing concern is the economic strain caused by a shrinking workforce. As a larger proportion of the population exits the labor market, governments face reduced tax revenues while simultaneously needing to fund expanded pension and healthcare systems. This imbalance has already created fiscal challenges in countries such as Japan, where the ratio of working-age adults to retirees has declined dramatically, straining public finances and slowing economic growth.

Additionally, an aging population places considerable pressure on healthcare systems. Older individuals typically require more frequent and specialized medical care, leading to increased demand for healthcare services and long-term care facilities. The resulting strain on medical infrastructure can lead to longer waiting times and reduced quality of care for the entire population.

In conclusion, while an aging population brings some benefits in terms of experience and community contribution, the economic and healthcare challenges it creates are substantial. These disadvantages, affecting both public finances and social services, outweigh the advantages, making this a trend that requires careful management and proactive policy responses.

*(274 words)*

**Band 8.0 features:**
- Clear thesis: disadvantages outweigh advantages
- Well-balanced structure: advantage → two strong disadvantages → conclusion
- Advanced vocabulary: "demographic shift," "fiscal challenges," "specialized medical care"
- Specific country example (Japan)
- Complex grammar: passive voice, relative clauses, conditional structures

## Essay 5: Work — Remote Work (Band 7.5)

**Prompt:** More and more people are working from home. Do the advantages of this trend outweigh the disadvantages?

**Sample Essay:**

The shift towards remote work has accelerated dramatically in recent years, particularly following global events that necessitated flexible working arrangements. While this transition presents certain challenges, I believe the advantages of working from home are more significant than the drawbacks.

The primary disadvantage of remote work is the potential for social isolation and the erosion of workplace culture. When employees are physically separated from colleagues, spontaneous interactions and informal mentoring opportunities are diminished. Additionally, the boundary between professional and personal life can become blurred, as the home environment serves dual purposes, potentially leading to overwork and burnout.

Despite these concerns, the benefits of remote work are substantial. The most significant advantage is the elimination of commuting, which not only saves employees time and money but also reduces traffic congestion and carbon emissions. Studies have shown that the average worker spends approximately 200 hours per year commuting, time that could be redirected towards productivity or personal well-being when working from home.

Furthermore, remote work enables organizations to access a global talent pool, unconstrained by geographical limitations. This allows companies to hire the most qualified candidates regardless of location, while employees benefit from greater flexibility to design their work schedules around personal circumstances. This flexibility has been shown to improve job satisfaction and, consequently, employee retention rates.

In conclusion, while remote work does present challenges related to isolation and work-life boundaries, the benefits of reduced commuting, environmental sustainability, and access to global talent make it a predominantly positive development. Organizations that implement thoughtful remote work policies, including regular virtual check-ins and clear communication protocols, can mitigate the disadvantages while maximizing the benefits.

*(289 words)*

**Band 7.5 features:**
- Clear thesis with balanced treatment
- Good vocabulary: "erosion of workplace culture," "blurred," "unconstrained by geographical limitations"
- Specific data reference ("200 hours per year")
- Complex structures: not only...but also, while clauses, passive voice
- Practical conclusion with actionable suggestion
