# IELTS Task 2 Essay Structure Guide

## Paragraph-by-Paragraph Template

### Introduction (2-3 sentences, ~40-50 words)

**Formula:** Paraphrase prompt + Thesis statement

**Paraphrasing techniques:**
- Synonyms: "important" → "crucial/significant/vital"
- Change word form: "develop" (verb) → "development" (noun)
- Change voice: active → passive
- Restructure: "A causes B" → "B is a consequence of A"

**Thesis patterns by essay type:**
- Opinion: "This essay will argue that [position] because [reason 1] and [reason 2]."
- Discussion: "While [view 1] has merit, I believe [view 2] is more compelling."
- Adv/Disadv: "Although [disadvantage], the advantages of [topic] are more significant."
- Problem/Solution: "This essay will explore the main causes of [problem] and propose viable solutions."
- Two-part: "This essay will address [Q1] and [Q2] in turn."

### Body Paragraph (4-6 sentences, ~70-90 words each)

**PEEL structure:**
```
P - Point (Topic sentence): State the main argument of this paragraph
E - Evidence/Example: Provide specific example, data, or scenario
E - Explanation: Explain how the evidence supports the point
L - Link: Concluding sentence linking back to the thesis or transitioning
```

**Topic sentence patterns:**
- "The primary reason why [argument] is that [reason]."
- "One compelling argument in favor of [view] is that [point]."
- "A further point worth considering is that [point]."
- "Another significant factor is [point]."
- "It can also be argued that [point]."

**Example introduction phrases:**
- "For instance, ..."
- "A case in point is ..."
- "This is exemplified by ..."
- "Take [example] as an illustration: ..."
- "Evidence of this can be seen in ..."

**Explanation phrases:**
- "This means that ..."
- "Consequently, ..."
- "As a result, ..."
- "This leads to ..."
- "The implication here is that ..."

**Concluding/linking sentences:**
- "Therefore, it is evident that [point]."
- "This demonstrates that [point]."
- "Having established [point], it is also important to consider [next point]."

### Conclusion (2-3 sentences, ~30-40 words)

**Formula:** Restate thesis (paraphrased) + Summarize key points + Final thought

**Patterns:**
- "In conclusion, although [counter-point], I believe [position] because [reason summary]."
- "To summarize, [point 1] and [point 2] demonstrate that [thesis]."
- "In conclusion, while [view 1], the [stronger view] because [reason]. [Final thought/prediction]."

**Final thought patterns:**
- "If [condition], then [outcome]."
- "Looking ahead, [prediction]."
- "Ultimately, [broad reflection]."

## Cohesion and Linking Devices

### Addition / Extension
- Furthermore / Moreover / In addition / Additionally / What is more
- Not only... but also... / Apart from this / Besides

### Contrast / Concession
- However / Nevertheless / Nonetheless / On the other hand
- Although / Even though / Despite the fact that / While
- Admittedly / Granted / It is true that

### Cause and Effect
- Therefore / Consequently / As a result / Hence / Thus
- This leads to / This results in / This brings about
- Because of / Due to / Owing to / On account of

### Examples and Illustration
- For example / For instance / Such as / Namely
- A case in point / To illustrate / As an illustration

### Sequencing / Ordering
- First and foremost / To begin with / Initially
- Secondly / Subsequently / Following this
- Finally / Last but not least / Ultimately

### Summarizing / Concluding
- In conclusion / To summarize / In summary
- On the whole / Taking everything into consideration / All things considered

### Comparison
- Similarly / Likewise / In the same way / Correspondingly
- By contrast / Conversely / On the contrary

## Word Count Management

- Minimum: 250 words (below this = penalty)
- Recommended: 270-300 words
- Maximum: ~320 words (don't write too much — risk of irrelevance and time pressure)

**Paragraph word allocation:**
- Introduction: ~40-50 words
- Body 1: ~80-90 words
- Body 2: ~80-90 words
- Conclusion: ~30-40 words
- Total: ~230-270 → Body 3 or longer bodies if needed

## Common Structure Mistakes to Avoid

1. **No clear thesis** — Introduction must state your position explicitly
2. **Topic sentence missing** — Each body paragraph needs one
3. **No examples** — Arguments without evidence score low on TR
4. **One-sided discussion** — For "discuss both views," must cover both sides
5. **New ideas in conclusion** — Conclusion summarizes, doesn't introduce new points
6. **Under-length** — Below 250 words = automatic penalty
7. **Over-reliance on template language** — "With the development of society..." is overused and penalized
8. **Memorized chunks** — Examiners detect and penalize memorized sentences
