# IELTS Task 1 Chart Types and Writing Strategies

## 1. Line Chart (折线图)

**What it shows:** Data changes over time (most common)

**Writing strategy:**
1. Introduction: paraphrase what the chart shows (variable, time period, units)
2. Overview: identify the general trend (increasing/decreasing/fluctuating/stable)
3. Details: describe specific trends, focusing on:
   - Highest and lowest points
   - Starting and ending values
   - Significant changes (sharp rises, sudden falls)
   - Where lines cross or converge
   - Periods of stability

**Sample sentences:**
- "The line chart illustrates the changes in [variable] from [year] to [year]."
- "[Category A] experienced a steady increase from [X] to [Y] over the period."
- "After reaching a peak of [X] in [year], [category] declined sharply to [Y]."
- "The trend for [category A] was upward, whereas [category B] showed a downward trajectory."
- "[Category A] overtook [category B] in [year], when [A] reached [X] compared to [B]'s [Y]."

**Key vocabulary:** rise, fall, increase, decrease, grow, decline, surge, plummet, fluctuate, peak, trough, plateau, level off, stabilize, trend upward/downward

## 2. Bar Chart (柱状图)

**What it shows:** Comparison of categories (may or may not be over time)

**Writing strategy:**
1. Introduction: paraphrase the chart (categories, time if applicable, units)
2. Overview: identify the dominant category/categories and overall pattern
3. Details: compare categories:
   - Highest and lowest bars
   - Group similar categories together
   - Note any surprising or notable differences
   - If time-based, describe changes within categories

**Sample sentences:**
- "The bar chart compares the [variable] across [N] categories in [year]."
- "[Category A] accounted for the largest proportion at [X], nearly double that of [category B]."
- "The figures for [category A] and [category B] were remarkably similar, at [X] and [Y] respectively."
- "[Category] showed a moderate increase from [X] to [Y], while [other category] remained relatively stable."
- "There was a significant disparity between [category A] and [category B], with the former being approximately [N] times greater."

## 3. Pie Chart (饼图)

**What it shows:** Proportions/percentages of a whole (often multiple pie charts for comparison)

**Writing strategy:**
1. Introduction: paraphrase what the pie chart(s) show
2. Overview: identify the largest and smallest segments, overall distribution
3. Details:
   - Describe the largest segments first
   - Compare segments across multiple pie charts (if applicable)
   - Note any segments that grew or shrank between time periods
   - Group smaller segments together ("the remaining [X]% was divided between...")

**Sample sentences:**
- "The pie chart illustrates the breakdown of [variable] into [N] categories."
- "[Category A] constituted the largest segment, accounting for [X]% of the total."
- "A comparison of the two pie charts reveals that [category A] grew from [X]% to [Y]%."
- "The proportion of [category] more than doubled from [X]% to [Y]%."
- "The remaining [X]% was shared among [category A], [category B], and [category C]."
- "[Category A] and [category B] together represented over half of the total at [X]%."

## 4. Table (表格)

**What it shows:** Data organized in rows and columns (multiple variables/dimensions)

**Writing strategy:**
1. Introduction: paraphrase what the table shows (variables, categories, time, units)
2. Overview: identify the overall pattern — highest/lowest across the table
3. Details:
   - Do NOT describe every cell — select the most significant
   - Compare across rows and columns
   - Identify the highest and lowest in each category
   - Note patterns (e.g., one country consistently highest across all metrics)
   - Group related data

**Sample sentences:**
- "The table provides information about [variable] across [N] categories."
- "As can be seen from the table, [category A] recorded the highest figure for [metric], at [X]."
- "[Category A] outperformed [category B] in terms of [metric], with figures of [X] and [Y] respectively."
- "The most striking feature is that [category] consistently ranked highest across all [N] metrics."
- "While [category A] saw a [X]% increase in [metric], [category B] experienced a decline of [Y]%."

## 5. Process Diagram (流程图)

**What it shows:** A step-by-step process (natural or man-made)

**Writing strategy:**
1. Introduction: paraphrase what the process shows
2. Overview: describe the overall process (number of stages, start to end)
3. Details:
   - Describe stages sequentially (usually chronologically)
   - Use passive voice (the process is described, not the doer)
   - Use sequencing words (first, next, then, subsequently, finally)
   - Describe what happens at each stage (input → process → output)

**Sample sentences:**
- "The diagram illustrates the process by which [product] is manufactured."
- "The process consists of [N] main stages, beginning with [stage 1] and ending with [final stage]."
- "First, [material] is [action] and then [action]."
- "Once [stage X] is complete, the [material] is transferred to [stage Y]."
- "At this stage, [substance] is added to [mixture], which causes [result]."
- "The final product is then [action] before being [action] for distribution."

**Key vocabulary:** is poured, is mixed, is heated, is cooled, is filtered, is transferred, is added, is removed, is combined, is processed, is packaged, is delivered

## 6. Map (地图)

**What it shows:** Changes to a place over time (usually 2-3 maps: past, present, and/or future)

**Writing strategy:**
1. Introduction: paraphrase what the maps show (place, time periods compared)
2. Overview: describe the overall transformation (e.g., from rural to urban, expanded, modernized)
3. Details:
   - Compare the maps systematically (north to south, or by area)
   - Describe what was added, removed, or changed
   - Use directional language (north, south, northeast, adjacent to, to the left of)
   - Use past tense for the old map, present for current, future for planned

**Sample sentences:**
- "The maps illustrate the changes that have taken place in [place] between [year 1] and [year 2]."
- "Overall, the area has been transformed from a [old state] into a [new state]."
- "In the north of the map, [old feature] was demolished and replaced by [new feature]."
- "To the east of [feature], a new [building/facility] was constructed."
- "The [road/river/path] that ran from [direction] to [direction] was extended/removed."
- "While [feature A] remained unchanged, [feature B] was significantly expanded."
- "The area previously occupied by [old feature] is now the site of [new feature]."

## 7. Multiple Charts (复合图)

**What it shows:** Two or more charts that are related (e.g., pie + bar, table + line)

**Writing strategy:**
1. Introduction: paraphrase what the charts collectively show
2. Overview: identify the overall trend across all charts
3. Details:
   - Describe Chart 1 first (its own paragraph)
   - Then describe Chart 2 (its own paragraph)
   - If possible, make connections between the charts
   - Don't force comparisons if the charts are about different things

**Sample sentences:**
- "The [chart type 1] and [chart type 2] provide complementary data on [topic]."
- "Turning to the [chart type 2], it can be seen that..."
- "While the [chart 1] shows [X], the [chart 2] reveals [Y]."
- "This correlates with the data presented in the [chart 1], where..."

## Tense Guide by Chart Type

| Chart Type | Time Reference | Tense |
|-----------|---------------|-------|
| Line chart | Past years (1990-2020) | Past simple |
| Line chart | Including future predictions | Past for past, future (will) for predictions |
| Bar chart | Specific year (no time change) | Present simple |
| Bar chart | Multiple years | Past simple |
| Pie chart | Specific year | Present simple |
| Pie chart | Multiple years | Past simple |
| Table | Current data | Present simple |
| Table | Historical data | Past simple |
| Process | Generic/universal | Present simple (passive) |
| Map | Past to present | Past simple (for old), present (for new) |
| Map | Present to future | Present for current, future (will be) for future |
